# Aligner Tracker — Marketing Strategy

## Purpose

Create a low-cost, focused marketing plan for Aligner Tracker that matches the product itself: simple, useful, privacy-conscious, and targeted at people who are actively wearing clear aligners.

This is a marketing document, not a product feature specification, so it belongs at:

`docs/marketing/marketing-strategy.md`

## Marketing Principle

> Reach people when they already have the problem, rather than trying to build a broad audience.

Aligner Tracker serves a narrow, high-intent audience. Early marketing should therefore prioritize:

1. people currently wearing clear aligners
2. orthodontists and dentists who see aligner patients every day
3. App Store search/discovery
4. existing personal networks and relevant online communities

Avoid broad paid advertising until the app has proven that its App Store page converts and that users find the product valuable.

## Core Positioning

Primary message:

> A fast, simple iPhone app for tracking clear-aligner wear time, tray progress, and reminders.

Supporting differentiators, when they are true in the released build:

- fast one-tap IN/OUT tracking
- useful tray progress at a glance
- reminders for aligner routines
- local-first core tracking
- no account required for core use
- focused specifically on aligner tracking rather than being a general dental app

Founder-story angle:

> Built by an aligner patient who wanted a simpler way to track treatment throughout the day.

This is useful because it makes the app feel like a solution created from a real daily problem instead of a generic software product.

Do not market unreleased features as if they already exist.

## Recommended Channel Priority

### 1. Orthodontist and Dentist Offices — Highest-Potential Offline Channel

This should be one of the primary launch channels.

Start with the user's own orthodontist and dentist offices. They already know the developer/patient and provide a low-friction way to learn whether professionals think the app is useful enough to mention to patients.

Recommended approach:

- briefly show the app to the orthodontist, dentist, treatment coordinator, or office manager
- explain that it was built during the developer's own aligner treatment
- ask whether they would be comfortable making a small card available to aligner patients
- ask for product feedback as well as distribution

Prefer small materials over a traditional large flyer:

- 5x7 countertop card
- half-sheet flyer if the office has a patient-information area
- business-card-sized handouts that can be added to an aligner starter packet

The handout should contain:

- app icon/name
- one-sentence value proposition
- 2–3 benefits maximum
- one clean app screenshot
- QR code
- "Available on the App Store"

Suggested headline:

> Wearing clear aligners? Make wear-time tracking easier.

Suggested body:

> Track daily IN/OUT time, follow your tray progress, and get helpful reminders with Aligner Tracker for iPhone.

Suggested credibility line:

> Built by an aligner patient for everyday aligner tracking.

Do not imply that a dentist or orthodontist endorses or recommends the app unless the practice has explicitly agreed to that language.

### 2. Personal Social Accounts — Start Before Launch

Begin posting before release, but treat this as a soft launch rather than an extended hype campaign.

The first post should explain:

- why the app exists
- that it came from the developer's own aligner experience
- one screenshot or short screen recording
- that launch is approaching
- how someone can volunteer to beta test, if beta testing is still useful

Good channels:

- Facebook / Instagram: strongest fit for friends, family, local contacts, and consumer sharing
- LinkedIn: useful for the builder/founder story and professional network, but probably weaker direct user acquisition
- TikTok / Reels: optional; useful only if creating short demos feels sustainable

Do not post generic "coming soon" messages repeatedly. Each post should contain something new:

- first public preview
- short tracker demo
- why the app was built
- a useful feature
- App Store approval / pre-order
- launch announcement
- meaningful post-launch update

### Recommended Pre-Launch Social Post Concept

> I've been wearing clear aligners and wanted a faster, simpler way to keep track of how long they're actually in each day, so I built one.
>
> Aligner Tracker is an iPhone app focused on quick IN/OUT tracking, tray progress, and useful reminders without making the process complicated.
>
> I'm getting it ready for the App Store now. If you're currently using clear aligners and would like to try it, send me a message.

Use a screenshot, short screen recording, or polished App Store-style image with the post.

## 3. App Store Page / App Store Optimization

The App Store page is the conversion point for nearly every channel, so it should be treated as a marketing asset rather than administrative metadata.

Prioritize:

- clear app name/subtitle
- strong first three screenshots
- screenshot text that explains benefits rather than simply labeling screens
- keywords centered on actual search intent such as aligner tracking, Invisalign-style wear tracking, retainer/aligner timers where appropriate, and tray tracking
- concise description focused on utility

The first screenshot should make the core value understandable without reading the full description.

After the app has meaningful traffic, use Apple's Product Page Optimization rather than guessing which screenshots or messages convert best.

Custom Product Pages can later support different campaigns, for example:

- general aligner tracking
- wear-time focused campaign
- orthodontist-office QR campaign

Keep this simple initially; one excellent default product page matters more than many variants.

## 4. Simple Web Landing Page

Use an existing developer web presence rather than creating a separate marketing system unless the app grows enough to justify one.

A simple Aligner Tracker landing page should include:

- app icon and name
- one-sentence explanation
- 3–4 key benefits
- screenshots
- App Store link / badge
- privacy-focused message
- support/contact link

Before launch, the primary CTA can be beta access or an App Store pre-order if one is configured.

After launch, the page should send people directly to the App Store.

The landing page also provides a stable URL for printed materials in case the App Store destination changes later.

## 5. Aligner Communities

Potential channels after the app is usable by the public:

- clear-aligner subreddits
- Facebook aligner / Invisalign communities
- orthodontic patient forums
- local community groups when genuinely relevant

Approach these as communities, not ad inventory.

- follow each group's self-promotion rules
- disclose that the poster built the app
- explain the problem it solved
- ask for feedback rather than pretending to be an unrelated user recommending it
- avoid repeatedly posting the same promotional message

A useful community post is closer to "I built this while going through aligner treatment; what am I missing?" than "Download my app."

## Launch Phases

### Phase 0 — Now / Pre-Launch

Goal: build a small group of interested users and collect feedback.

Do now:

- create one strong social announcement
- prepare a short demo video or polished screenshot
- create/update an Aligner Tracker landing page
- invite current aligner users in the personal network to beta test
- show the app privately to the user's orthodontist and dentist
- begin drafting office marketing material, but do not print a large quantity yet

If the release date becomes firm and the app is eligible, consider enabling App Store pre-order so pre-launch promotion has a direct conversion action.

### Phase 1 — Launch

Goal: get the first real users from high-intent sources.

Do at launch:

- launch announcement across personal socials
- update landing page to the live App Store link
- give the user's orthodontist/dentist a small stack of cards
- ask beta testers and early users to share the app if they genuinely like it
- participate in a small number of relevant aligner communities where promotion is permitted
- make sure the App Store screenshots and description clearly communicate the core value

Target milestone:

> First 100 users from organic, attributable sources before spending meaningfully on advertising.

### Phase 2 — Validate Channels

Goal: identify what actually produces downloads and retained users.

Compare:

- personal social traffic
- orthodontist-office traffic
- web referrals
- App Store search
- community referrals

Once App Store campaign links are available, use different links/QR codes for major channels, for example:

- `social-launch`
- `ortho-office`
- `dentist-office`
- `alecbytes-site`
- `community-post`

Apple's App Store Connect analytics can measure campaign-driven downloads and downstream usage without requiring a third-party analytics SDK in the app.

Do not over-instrument small campaigns; Apple withholds very small campaign datasets for privacy, so directional results matter more than precise attribution at tiny scale.

### Phase 3 — Scale What Works

Only after organic validation:

- approach additional local orthodontists
- create a polished office starter pack
- improve App Store assets based on conversion data
- test Product Page Optimization
- consider Apple Ads for high-intent search terms
- consider creator partnerships with aligner-focused creators if a natural fit appears

Avoid broad Meta/TikTok advertising until there is evidence that paid acquisition can convert economically.

## Orthodontist Office Outreach Strategy

The initial goal is not "sell the office." It is:

> Get one orthodontic practice to believe the app is useful enough to make available to its patients.

Suggested conversation:

1. Explain that the app came from the developer's own experience using aligners.
2. Demo the tracker in 30–60 seconds.
3. Ask whether the practice sees value for patients who struggle to keep track of wear time.
4. Ask what the practice would want changed before recommending it.
5. Offer a small stack of free patient cards once the app is live.

If this works with the first practice, create a repeatable outreach package before contacting many more offices.

Possible later office kit:

- 5x7 countertop display
- small stack of patient cards
- QR code linked to an office campaign
- one-page staff explanation

Do not build provider accounts, office dashboards, referral systems, or orthodontist integrations just to support marketing. Those are separate product decisions and should exist only if users/providers demonstrate a real need.

## Content Ideas

Use a small number of repeatable content themes:

- "Why I built Aligner Tracker"
- 15-second IN/OUT tracking demo
- "How I keep track of daily aligner wear time"
- tray-change workflow demo
- reminder feature demo
- progress/statistics feature demo when available
- app design / development milestones
- seasonal-theme previews as lightweight visual content once those features ship

The product should remain the content. Avoid creating a separate content-marketing workload that becomes harder to maintain than the app itself.

## What Not to Do Yet

- broad paid social advertising
- influencer campaigns with upfront spend
- expensive printed materials
- large email marketing infrastructure
- third-party analytics SDKs solely for marketing attribution
- affiliate/referral systems
- orthodontist dashboards
- complicated lead funnels
- daily social posting requirements

## Initial Success Measures

Keep early measurement simple:

- first-time downloads
- App Store product-page conversion rate
- App Store search vs referral sources
- campaign-link downloads once available
- App Store ratings/reviews and qualitative feedback
- subscription conversion if/when Pro is live
- which orthodontist/dentist offices voluntarily keep or request more cards

Early marketing success is finding one or two repeatable acquisition channels, not maximizing impressions.

## Recommended Immediate Actions

1. Make one personal "I built this because I'm using aligners myself" social post now.
2. Create a simple landing page or dedicated Aligner Tracker section on the existing developer site.
3. Ask the user's orthodontist for 5 minutes to demo the app and get feedback before launch.
4. Design a 5x7 office card and a business-card-sized patient handout, but initially print only a small test batch.
5. Finish the App Store product page so every external marketing channel lands on a convincing page.
6. At launch, use source-specific App Store campaign links/QR codes as soon as App Store Connect makes campaign links available.
7. Review results after the first ~100 organic users before spending meaningful money on advertising.

## Open Decisions

Decide closer to launch:

- whether to use an App Store pre-order period
- whether pre-launch users should join TestFlight by direct invitation or another lightweight signup method
- exact public positioning of free vs Pro functionality
- final office handout format: 5x7, half-sheet, business card, or a small combination
- whether to create a dedicated Aligner Tracker domain later or keep marketing under the existing developer site

These decisions do not block beginning the pre-launch marketing work now.
